# Product Page Playbook

Apply this on top of the core principles in SKILL.md when designing or reviewing an e-commerce product detail page, product card, quick-view or add-to-cart step.

A good product page answers three questions in one glance, in order: **What is it? Can I trust it? What will it cost me?** Then it makes buying effortless.

## Before you start

Identify how the product is sold: per item, by weight, by size, by pack or by subscription. The unit of sale changes the image, the title, the quantity control and how the price is shown.

## Product imagery

Choose or specify images that:
- **Have one clear focal point**: the product, not hands, props or a frame filled with repeated items.
- **Match what is sold.** Sold by weight or in a pack → show a representative quantity, not one unit.
- **Feel honest for the category.** Fresh or natural goods need natural, real-looking images, not artificial colored backdrops.
- **Fit the catalog system.** Same clean background, lighting and framing as every other product, so listings and grids stay calm. A strong lifestyle shot with its own background can be the wrong choice if it breaks the catalog.

Navigation icons over the hero image (back, share, favorite, cart) need containers so they work on bright products too.

## The top of the page

- **Title** names the product only ("Organic Strawberries", not "Organic Strawberries 1kg") when the user can change the quantity. A fixed quantity in the title becomes misleading.
- **Rating and review count** sit directly under or beside the title. Reviews are one of the strongest trust signals in e-commerce.
- **Price** appears early, near title and rating, so the first screen shows what it is, how good it is and how much it costs.
- **No "Price:" label.** A currency-formatted number is obviously a price.
- **Discount badges** are one short phrase ("20% off"), no icon, no redundant words.
- **Category labels** in small caps get letter spacing.

## Description and features

- Description: comfortable line height, slightly softer contrast than the title.
- Feature or benefit icons (organic, delivery, freshness…): one icon style, restrained color, tight spacing, so they inform without dominating.
- Dividers between sections stay subtle.

## Add to cart

- **Add to cart** is the primary action: accent color, sentence case, refined rather than aggressive.
- **Quantity selector sits directly beside it.** Users choose an amount, then add it.
- **Unit inside the selector** ("1 kg", "2 pcs").
- **Total price inside the button** ("Add to cart · $8.40"), updated live as quantity changes.

## Experience improvements

Once the basics are right:

- **Scrollable content sheet (mobile).** Details sit in a card with rounded top corners and a soft shadow that slides up over the hero image on scroll. Once the image scrolls away, the product title moves into a sticky top bar so users never lose context.
- **Sticky bottom action bar.** Quantity and add to cart stay visible while scrolling, because people often decide after reading details, reviews or related products.
- **Quantity presets.** One-tap chips for common amounts (e.g. 500 g / 1 kg / 2 kg), ideally based on real purchase data, with the custom stepper kept for flexibility.

## Product page checks

Add these to the general audit:

1. Hero-image icons legible on a bright product image.
2. Image has one focal point, shows the sold quantity and matches the catalog spec.
3. Title contains no adjustable quantity.
4. Rating is beside or under the title.
5. Price is on the first screen, without a "Price:" label.
6. Badges are one short phrase.
7. Quantity selector is next to add to cart, with the unit shown.
8. Button shows the live total and is sentence case.

## Stress cases

- A bright product image and a dark one.
- A long product name.
- A large price with a discount.
- No reviews yet.
- One product sold per unit and one sold by weight.
